S-400

We draw to the code the reviewer is holding.

South Florida isn't a generic jurisdiction. A set that would sail through in another state gets red-lined here on the first pass. These are the criteria we design to before anything reaches a plans examiner.

  • FBC 8th Edition
  • HVHZ Miami-Dade & Broward
  • NOA product approval
  • TAS 201 / 202 / 203
  • ASCE 7-22 wind
  • FEMA VE / AE / BFE
  • 50% rule, storm repairs

  • Wind

    Design pressures from the site's actual wind speed and exposure, with the zone breakdown a reviewer expects to find on the sheet.

  • Flood

    Base flood elevation drives foundation type, finished floor and flood vent calculations. We check the FIRM panel before we draw.

  • Product approval

    Openings and assemblies specified against current NOA numbers, so the submittal doesn't stall on a lapsed approval.

  • Coastal and corrosion

    Fastener and coating specs appropriate to the exposure, which is what gets questioned on waterfront work.

A-500

How a project runs.

Five stages. The fifth is the one most firms don't include.

  1. 01

    Site survey

    An inspector measures and photographs the property. For some scopes we can work from your documents instead; we'll tell you which.

  2. 02

    Proposal

    Scope, deliverables, timeline and fee. The proposal is the contract. 50% to start.

  3. 03

    Production

    Assigned to a dedicated design team with a named lead. You get one point of contact and a delivery date, not a queue position.

  4. 04

    Delivery

    Signed and sealed set ready for submittal, plus whatever the jurisdiction wants alongside it.

  5. 05

    Permitting support

    You submit. If plan review returns comments, we correct and resubmit. Included.
